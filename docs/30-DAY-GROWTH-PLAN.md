# Rupesh's 30-day LinkedIn growth plan

Run dates: October 10 to November 8, 2026, inclusive. All times are Asia/Calcutta (IST).

The aim is to become a recognizable design voice among early-stage founders and product leaders, generate consulting conversations, and build relationships with teams hiring a founding designer. LinkedIn Top Voice is an editorial designation. This plan cannot guarantee a badge or its timing; the 30-day result will be assessed through useful conversations, relevant audience growth, and opportunities.

The October 18 revision follows the [Top Voice evidence and execution plan](TOP-VOICE-PLAN.md). It shifts future morning slots to attributed product screenshots and verified current design reading, reduces future consulting invitations to Tuesdays, and preserves the historical slots and receipts. The [current product gallery](../resources/linkedin/current-design/README.md) records source provenance.

The owner requested two posts every day, including weekends. This is a 30-day publishing experiment with sessions at 09:00 and 18:00, with current design commentary in the morning and a focused opinion or discussion in the evening. A due slot is scheduled five minutes ahead, so an on-time session publishes at approximately 09:05 or 18:05 IST. This cadence overrides the imported planner's default of three to five posts a week.

The connected Publora Pro account was verified with a 5,000-post monthly allowance per connection, analytics enabled, and unrestricted queue/horizon fields. That accommodates the planned 60 slots under the observed capacity. The runner still checks current capacity before publishing and retains the original October 13 and 14 morning seeds rather than duplicating them.

Confirmed background: creative director, product designer, startup founder, and brand strategy experience. Confirmed goals: consulting clients, a stronger personal brand, and founding designer opportunities. The calendar uses practical advice and proposed opinions from those disciplines. ## Daily operating brief

1. Publish the morning and evening slots below. Each post makes one useful argument, explains a concrete decision, and uses plain language. Use no fabricated anecdotes, numerical results, endorsements, client names, or unsupported quotes.
2. Aim for 30–40 relevant public actions on other people's posts per day, with ceilings of 20 substantive comments and 20 likes. Spread them across the five-hour daily window from 17:00 to 22:00 IST, with at most one like and one substantive comment every fifteen minutes. Repeated five-minute checks share each slot's allowance, and missed slots expire. Skip weak matches even when that leaves the target unfilled. Likes express agreement with material actually read; each comment adds an example, a decision criterion, or a specific question. Cache profile discovery for 24 hours and restrict eligible source posts to the past seven days.
3. Prioritize comments on Rupesh's own published posts when the connected read service makes them available. Answer the actual question, acknowledge useful disagreement, and continue genuine discussion. Deduplicate replies by comment identity and preserve a durable pending queue. Monitor up to 60 verified own posts in rotating batches of up to five; handle at most 20 replies per run and 1,000 per day, separately from the outbound public-post target.
4. Keep sales invitations on the calendar below: one consulting invitation on Tuesday and one founding designer invitation on Friday per future full seven-day block. Other posts stop when the point is complete; they do not need a lesson or a closing question. Do not turn comments on somebody else's post into pitches.
5. Record publication receipts and interaction results. An uncertain write is not repeated automatically. A skipped action is a quality or capability outcome, not a reason to manufacture activity.

Target distribution for outbound comments: approximately 70% peers and early-stage builders, 20% established product/design voices, and 10% prospective clients or hiring founders. Prefer public conversations about product strategy, onboarding, brand promises, design hiring, customer experience, and design trade-offs. Large-audience accounts alone are not a sufficient target pool; rotate through relevant founders and designers, including smaller accounts where a thoughtful exchange is possible.

The five-hour window describes the schedule for public actions. It does not simulate continuous online presence or claim five hours of human work. Scheduling delays, relevance checks, and the unchanged read budget can leave slots unused. Replies to available comments remain independent of this window and can run throughout the day.

The workflow checks every five minutes, but instant comment replies are not established. Publora's comment-count statistics are cached for two hours and LinkedIn reporting can lag up to 24 hours. A changed count or daily reconciliation triggers a fresh paid read, with a six-hour fallback when a statistic cannot be verified. Available pagination and queued replies preserve work across sessions, subject to the reader's actual results.

Cost plan: keep `APIFY_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD` at its default $4, using the existing $5 monthly Apify Free-plan credit pool for post generation, weekly analysis, and necessary public reads. Use the verified local Qwen3 4B CPU model for comments/replies and as the post fallback when the budget guard blocks paid inference. Publora's published one-account Pro price is $5.99 monthly; the account is verified as Pro, while the invoice was not inspected. No new paid DM service, pay-as-you-go activation, or higher usage budget is enabled. DMs and profile updates remain inactive because no supported connection within the total $5–7 target has been verified.

## Measurement and adaptation

The October 18 audit of the latest ten published posts reports 714 impressions, four reactions, zero comments and zero reshares. Impressions are not unique reach, recent counts can lag, and follower count, profile views, consulting pipeline and role pipeline remain unknown. Collect a baseline from an available authorized source; mark unavailable fields as unknown. Publishing and interaction receipts can establish an activity baseline without pretending to measure reach. The upgraded Publora Pro connection exposes analytics, but only returned, verified values can become evidence; an available feature does not itself establish a baseline.

| Measure | What counts | Review cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Publishing delivery | Verified published post IDs, scheduled items, deferrals, and failed/uncertain writes | Daily |
| Meaningful public discussion | Relevant two-way exchanges with distinct people; a like alone does not count | Daily; weekly summary |
| Consulting signal | A person explicitly discusses a relevant problem or asks about working together | Weekly |
| Qualified consulting conversation | A verified discussion that establishes the problem, decision-maker, and possible fit; interest is not a booked contract | Weekly |
| Founding designer signal | A relevant founder or hiring team explicitly discusses a role or design mandate | Weekly |
| Audience fit | Verified participants' publicly stated roles or interests match founders, product leaders, or design peers; leave uncertain classifications unclassified | Weekly |
| Reach and follower change | Values returned by an authorized source, measured against the same baseline | Weekly, when available |

Review points: October 16, 23, 30, and November 6, with the final review on November 8. Use actual discussions to select better targets, strengthen useful arguments, and change angles that repeat. Keep the requested publishing times during the experiment unless the owner changes the cadence. Do not claim that two posts per day caused a result without evidence.

The automation prepares and executes routine drafts, reads, comments, likes, and eligible replies within its connected capabilities and account limits. Missing private information, unavailable API features, and a request that would create a commercial commitment are recorded as exceptions. A public request about consulting can receive a contextual acknowledgment; the system does not invent a price, accept a project, or imply a meeting is booked.
